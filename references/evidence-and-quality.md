# Evidence, citations, and quality control

Read this reference whenever the work contains external facts, literature, cases, datasets, calculations, citations, or source links.

## Source policy

- Search public sources when research improves the result. Prefer primary, official, original, or otherwise authoritative sources for the claim at hand.
- Never fabricate a publication, author, date, DOI, statistic, case, quotation, URL, access result, or page number.
- Follow the required or established citation style. With neither, use numeric superscripts and an end reference list. Cite only accessible material when full text is unavailable. A source reporting worldwide growth cannot support a China-specific claim.
- Preserve original-source links where the format permits. Link to the actual source, not a search-results page or an unverifiable aggregator.
- Distinguish sourced fact, calculation, synthesis, inference, and recommendation. Phrase each at the strength its evidence supports.

## Claim-by-claim verification

For every material source-dependent claim, verify:

1. **Existence:** the source and link resolve to the claimed work or data.
2. **Support:** the cited passage or dataset actually supports the adjacent claim; do not cite by topic resemblance.
3. **Scope:** population, geography, period, method, sample, and definition match the wording.
4. **Metric:** unit, currency, nominal/real basis, percentage versus percentage point, denominator, aggregation, and rounding are explicit and consistent.
5. **Calculation:** inputs, formula, transformations, and result reconcile. Preserve enough provenance to reproduce it.
6. **Placement:** the marker is attached to the precise claim it supports, and references are complete and consistently formatted.

If a source cannot be verified, replace it with a verified source, narrow or remove the claim, or flag the gap outside the clean artifact. Never silently keep a plausible-looking citation.

## Visual evidence

- Use charts only when they make comparison, trend, composition, distribution, or relationship clearer.
- Label axes, units, dates, denominators, samples, and transformations. Start axes and truncate ranges only when analytically justified and visually explicit.
- Keep chart encodings faithful to magnitude; avoid decorative scale, misleading area/volume, cherry-picked windows, or mismatched baselines.
- Put the true source and clickable original link near the figure or in the required reference location, using the same marker as the supported content.

## QA record

Keep a compact internal ledger of material claims, source location, support check, scope/metric check, and action taken. The final review note may summarize this work, but the formal artifact should remain clean unless the requested genre requires an audit appendix.
