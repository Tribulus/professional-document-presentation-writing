# Document workflow

Read this reference for papers, reports, research writing, business documents, product/operations documents, or substantive prose revisions.

## Plan and draft

1. Read the assignment, rubric, template, source material, and prior confirmed choices before deciding what is missing.
2. Extract the audience, purpose, deliverable, required sections, length, deadline-relevant scope, source rules, and formatting constraints. Do not ask the user to repeat them.
3. In exploration mode, present 2–4 distinct directions with a framing or research question, evidence needs, and tradeoffs. Include open fields for **My idea** and **Other requirements (optional)** such as language, word count, page count, citation style or delivery format.
4. Confirm direction and outline only when they are genuinely unsettled. Otherwise draft directly.
5. Build each substantive section around a clear claim or purpose, the mechanism or reasoning, appropriate evidence, and the implication. Do not inflate length with repeated framing.

## Style and integrity

- Delete formulaic caveats such as “this only discusses X and does not represent all X” unless the boundary directly affects interpretation.
- Keep necessary scope conditions close to the affected claim. Put broader research limitations in one coherent limitations section when the genre permits.
- Preserve uncertainty that exists in the evidence; do not manufacture certainty, results, personal experience, fieldwork, or organizational impact.
- Mark missing evidence or unresolved placeholders visibly in a working draft. Do not turn placeholders into asserted facts in the clean version.
- In Word documents and reports, use a labeled figure or table placeholder by default when a visual aids understanding. Include its purpose, content or data, suggested form, and source needs. Produce the finished visual when the user requests it.

## Typography and structure

Follow the governing requirement for body size and spacing. Where it is silent, choose readable settings appropriate to the document. Check for orphaned headings, unnecessary blank lines and skipped heading levels. Keep Word headings and figure placeholders editable.

Unless a governing template says otherwise:

- Chinese text: Microsoft YaHei and full-width Chinese punctuation.
- English text: Times New Roman and half-width English punctuation.
- Mixed Chinese/English text: choose punctuation, spacing, and font by local linguistic context; do not apply a crude global substitution.
- Use automatic multilevel heading numbering: `1`, `1.1`, `1.1.1`. Use real heading styles so numbering, navigation, and tables of contents remain maintainable.
- Keep captions, notes, references, page elements, spacing, and indentation consistent.

## Final review

Check, in order of consequence:

- meaning and conclusion fidelity;
- factual and evidentiary support;
- argument structure and transitions;
- removal of padding, redundant qualifications, and duplicated conclusions;
- terminology, grammar, punctuation, and mixed-language typography;
- heading numbering, references, captions, tables/figures, pagination, and requested output format.

Return the clean document separately from a short review note that lists material assumptions, verified fixes, and any unresolved evidence or format limitation.
