# Presentation workflow

Read this reference for presentation strategy, slide outlines, PPT generation or editing, charts/diagrams, and speaker notes.

## Outline gate

Before generating a formal deck, provide a deck-level outline and a page plan. For every slide include:

- slide number and action-oriented title;
- core message and essential content;
- presentation form or layout;
- suggested chart, diagram, icon, or image when it adds information;
- suggested speaking time.

Also label each slide's planned density (low, medium or high) and flag data or sources still to be verified. For a planned visual, describe its job, what it must show and whether it can be drawn natively.

At the outline stage also provide:

- total presentation time and any known deductions for video, live demo, and Q&A;
- overall density choice: **More text**, **Moderate (default)**, or **More concise**;
- one free-form **Adjustment notes (optional)** field.

Wait for confirmation before formal generation when direction, structure, or visual density is still open. When the user explicitly authorizes direct execution or supplies a settled structure, keep this gate lightweight: state the inferred outline and density, then proceed without manufacturing a new approval round.

- **More text:** Add relevant background, mechanisms, case explanations and evidence.
- **Moderate (default):** Use a structured layout that combines charts or diagrams where helpful with the analysis needed to interpret them. Calibrate density using the [layout guide](moderate-density.md).
- **More concise:** Keep key points and evidence visible and move some spoken explanation into Speaker Notes.

Vary density by slide function and allow per-slide overrides in the adjustment field. Regroup content and clarify hierarchy first. Remove repetition or split when the page limit permits. Preserve linked comparisons and readable type. Avoid font scaling, mechanical cuts or extra whitespace as substitutes for editing.

## Slide construction

- Make the deck substantial but scannable. One slide may contain multiple supporting points when hierarchy makes the main message obvious.
- At outline stage, propose charts, flowcharts and images. In the formal deck, build feasible native charts and editable vector diagrams with adjustable text, shapes and connectors. Resolve planned visuals before delivery and disclose any material asset still unavailable.
- Give every image, icon and diagram a role in explaining, comparing, presenting evidence or aiding understanding. Omit visuals that merely fill space. Use raster images for complex physical subjects, documentary evidence, or visuals that cannot be faithfully reconstructed.
- Do not let generated imagery, area, perspective, color, or icon size imply unsupported quantitative differences.
- Caption visuals directly with what they show and any conditions needed to interpret them. Remove empty caveats and defensive phrasing from slides, captions and visual briefs.
- Follow the user's explicit template or design requirements first, then the project's established visual conventions. If neither applies, choose a design suited to the topic and audience. Apply a coherent type system, palette, grid, hierarchy, and page numbering. Use an optional header or progress indicator when it helps orientation.
- Place small but legible true references at the slide foot, with matching markers and clickable original links. Check that every marker maps to the correct claim or visual.
- Verify chart axes, units, denominators, date ranges, sorting, aggregation, and data labels.

Check that chart data and labels remain editable, connectors attach to relevant nodes, and comparable elements can be restyled. Use full-slide rasterization only when explicitly requested. Verify arrow direction and line-style meanings against the evidence. Label modeled assumptions and calculated results, distinguish documented paths from schematic ones, and identify generated diagrams as illustrative when needed. If references crowd the footer, keep readable short citations with links there and put complete entries in an appendix.

Check that graphic relationships do not imply unverified business steps, causal links or data conclusions.

## Timing

Allocate speaking time by conceptual complexity, evidence load, audience difficulty, and transition needs—not equally by slide count. Deduct video, live demonstration, interaction, and Q&A before distributing speaking time. Under a strict maximum, plan the spoken deck to finish with roughly 5–10% buffer.

Check that per-slide times sum to the planned spoken duration and that dense explanation slides receive enough time. If the outline cannot fit credibly, reduce scope or redesign rather than promising an impossible pace.

Meet any stated minimum as well as the maximum. Reconcile the final notes against the outline and overall schedule, counting videos, demos, interactions and Q&A once. Estimate requested scripts using a realistic speaking rate for the language and presenter.

## Native speaker notes

Every slide in the formal deck must contain native Speaker Notes with:

- a structured speaking outline;
- the core explanation or interpretation not obvious from the slide;
- a natural transition into or out of the slide;
- the suggested speaking time.

Notes should help a presenter speak naturally; do not mechanically copy slide text. Keep notes synchronized whenever slide content, order, evidence, or timing changes. Write a full verbatim script only when the user asks for one.

Adapt the note structure to the slide. For a roadmap, explain stages and decision gates. For a chart, guide the audience through reading it, interpreting the result and drawing the implication. For a comparison, explain dimensions, differences and significance. Keep evidence the audience needs on the slide.

Verify notes in the finished deck. If the output system cannot create native notes, explicitly disclose that limitation before delivery and provide the notes in the closest usable separate format; never claim that visible slide text or a notes appendix is native Speaker Notes.

## Final deck review

Render the actual deck and inspect every slide for text overflow, overlaps, margins, alignment, font substitution, contrast, hierarchy, chart labels, icon placement and footer legibility. Test source links in the editable deck. Fix defects, rerender and reinspect affected slides before delivery. Check message flow, chart integrity, total timing, notes presence and synchronization in the final version. Keep the clean deck separate from the review summary.
